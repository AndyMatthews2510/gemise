Module 1: The Engineer as Applicant & Communicator (Weeks 1–4)

Focuses on articulating technical expertise, professional agency, and resilience across academic fellowship panels and corporate engineering interviews.

Handout 1.2: Competency Formula

FORMULA: [Impact Verb] + [Technical Tool/Method] + [Quantified Output/Scope] + [Systemic Impact]

EXAMPLE: "Architected a ROS-based path-planning algorithm in Python for a miniature AGV, 
reducing localization latency by 18% under dynamic obstacle constraints."

Handout 1.3: STAR-L Framework Organizer

Element Time % Strategic Objective & Signposting
[S] Situation 15% Context, operational baseline, project constraints.
[T] Task 15% Your specific engineering mandate and the failure/bottleneck that occurred.
[A] Action 40% Diagnostic steps, engineering trade-offs evaluated, and systematic execution.
[R] Result 15% Quantified technical outcome and operational performance data.
[L] Learning 15% Reflection on technical failure; institutionalized standard operating procedures (ABET 4).

Module 2: Professional Collaboration & Strategic Negotiation (Weeks 5–8)

Equips students to chair technical sprint reviews, manage interdisciplinary conflict, and execute principled bilateral and multi-party commercial negotiations.

C1 Negotiation Discourse Cheat Sheet

Conditional Bargaining

  • "Assuming your team can guarantee a 48-hour turn-around, we could adjust our delivery milestones."
  • "We can concede on lead times provided that intermediate testing costs are co-funded."

Diplomatic Gatekeeping

  • "That is a critical algorithmic variable, but it falls outside today's architectural scope."
  • "Let’s park that edge-case in the parking lot and refocus on component lead times."

Assessment 2 Scenario: Project Nexus Crisis

Incident Brief: 10 automated electric micro-shuttles operating on a campus pilot experience battery thermal runaway warnings during 40°C heat trials. The Hardware, Software, Commercial, and Regulatory leads must resolve the impasse in 45 minutes and sign a binding Memorandum of Understanding (MoU).

Module 3: The Unified Engineering Innovation Proposal (Weeks 9–11)

Guides multidisciplinary teams in developing a 2,500-word formal engineering dossier that meets research grant criteria while targeting commercial competitions.

Unified Dossier Architecture (2,500 Words)

SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & STRATEGIC CONTEXT (~350 words)
├── 1.1 Executive Abstract (Problem, Novel Intervention, Performance Metric)
└── 1.2 Societal & UN SDG Alignment (Target 9.4, 9.5, 12.2, or 12.5 metrics)

SECTION 2: TECHNICAL DESIGN & METHODOLOGY (~1,000 words) [Academic Rigor Focus]
├── 2.1 State of the Art & Technical Gap (Prior art and patent limitations)
├── 2.2 Multidisciplinary System Architecture (CAD, Schematics, Governing Equations)
└── 2.3 Prototyping, Validation & Experimental Protocol (V&V testbeds)

SECTION 3: COMMERCIAL VIABILITY & COMPETITION STRATEGY (~650 words) [Venture Focus]
├── 3.1 Market Problem, Personas & Competitive Positioning
├── 3.2 Bill of Materials (BOM) & Prototyping Budget Justification
└── 3.3 Target Competition Roadmap (e.g., Dyson Award, MIT CleanTech)

SECTION 4: ETHICAL, REGULATORY & RISK MANAGEMENT (~500 words) [ABET 4]
├── 4.1 Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA Matrix: S × O × D = RPN)
└── 4.2 Environmental Lifecycle Analysis, Recyclability & IP Strategy

Module 4: Persuasive Pitching & Venture Defense (Weeks 12–14)

Prepares teams to deliver high-density 10-minute presentations and defend their technical validation choices against critical panel cross-examination.

The 10-Minute Slide Architecture: 10 slides maximum. Slides must prioritize block diagrams, telemetry graphs, and BOM breakdown tables over dense text paragraphs.
